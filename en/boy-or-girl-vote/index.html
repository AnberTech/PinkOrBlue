Boy or girl vote: how to run gender reveal voting

Everyone has a hunch. Here's how to make the boy or girl vote and guessing games part of your party, and how to keep every vote a secret until the reveal.

Why a boy or girl vote is so much fun

Before the big reveal, almost everyone has a gut feeling. Some trust a dream, some just "know," and older relatives often bring old wives' tales that get the whole family talking. These are only for fun; for reliable information about the baby's sex, ask your doctor. A vote turns all those hunches into part of the party. Everyone picks a side, the wait turns into a friendly contest, and the reveal becomes the moment you learn not only the result but also who got it right. Even guests who rarely speak up in the family group chat suddenly have an opinion.

Team Pink or Team Blue?

The favorite way to vote is to split guests into two teams. In PinkOrBlue, a guest taps the link, types their name and picks Boy or Girl, which puts them on Team Pink or Team Blue. Anyone who changes their mind can switch their vote any time before the reveal. In the party room, every guest appears as a balloon, and you can tell who has voted from who hasn't, so you know who you're still waiting for. After the reveal, the results screen shows both teams side by side and how many people called it. Sometimes the teams even split the family right down the middle.

Guessing games: birth date, weight and first letter

Adding a few guessing games to the boy or girl question keeps the fun going. PinkOrBlue offers guests three optional extra guesses: the birth date, the birth weight in grams, and the first letter of the baby's name. The person who knows the result, whether a parent or the secret keeper, doesn't vote Boy or Girl but can still make these three guesses, which appear next to everyone else's on the results screen. You can save all the guesses as a PDF. Opening that list after the baby arrives to see who came closest to the date and weight turns into a second round of fun months later.

Keeping the votes secret until the reveal

Half the thrill of a vote is not knowing who picked what until the very end. When votes are public, guests start following the crowd, and the first few votes sway the rest. In PinkOrBlue, who voted what stays secret until the reveal: until it starts, each guest sees only their own vote. The party room shows only who has voted and who hasn't yet, by name, never the vote itself. The result is protected the same way and doesn't show on guests' screens until the reveal starts. That way, everyone votes on their own hunch, and the surprise holds until the big moment.

Who called it at the reveal?

When the balloon pops, the result appears, followed by the names of everyone who guessed right. A guest who got it right sees "You called it!" on their own screen, and if nobody did, the screen says so too. On the results screen, guests can leave a message for the family and, now that they know, suggest a name. If you like, promise a small, symbolic prize to the winners, like dessert at the next family get-together. Presenting the vote as a shared game rather than a contest lets the guests who guessed wrong enjoy the surprise just as much.

Frequently asked questions

Can I change my vote after I've voted?

Yes. Until the reveal starts, you can change your Boy or Girl pick and your extra guesses. Once it begins, votes are final; the results screen shows the names of those who called it, each team's vote count and the guesses of anyone who added extra predictions.

Can I see what other guests voted?

No. Until the reveal starts, each guest sees only their own vote. The party room shows who has voted and who hasn't yet; at the reveal, the names of those who called it and each team's vote count come out.

Can the parents vote too?

The person who knows the result, meaning the host or the secret keeper, doesn't vote Boy or Girl but can guess the birth date, weight and first letter of the name. Parents who leave the result to a secret keeper can vote like everyone else.

Is there a reliable way to predict boy or girl?

The old wives' tales passed around in families are just for fun and don't decide anything. Talk to your doctor about how and when you can learn the baby's sex, and use the vote to enjoy everyone's guesses.

How can I see how many people have voted?

The party room shows how many people have voted and how many are on the page right now. Every guest appears as a balloon; tap a balloon to see whether that person has voted yet.
