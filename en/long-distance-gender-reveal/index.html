How to do a long distance gender reveal

Grandma may live in another city and your cousin in another country. Here's how to plan a reveal so everyone far away shares the big moment with you, all at once.

Bringing faraway family into the big moment

In many families, the people we love are spread across different cities and even different countries. That often means the baby's sex is shared in a phone call or a photo dropped into the group chat; the excitement is there, but the moment is gone. A virtual gender reveal closes that distance: everyone joins the same room from their own screen, watches the same countdown and learns the result together. That's exactly what PinkOrBlue is for. Anyone in another city or country joins the same room by tapping the link, with no travel, tickets or shared venue needed.

Time zones and choosing the date

When your family lives in different time zones, one reveal time won't suit everyone perfectly. Start by noting where the people who matter most live and work out their local time. Look for a window that doesn't land at midnight for one person or in the middle of a workday for another; weekends usually give you more room. In PinkOrBlue, the reveal time is shown on each guest's screen in their own device's time, and the countdown runs to the same moment for everyone. Still, mentioning the time difference in your invite message makes sure nobody gets ready at the wrong hour.

A live reveal, all at once

In a long distance party, the most valuable thing is a shared moment. When you start the reveal in PinkOrBlue, everyone with the page open sees the same thing: guests tap their screens to blow up a giant balloon together, and when it pops, the result opens on everyone's screen together, though connections can add a slight delay. If the host plays the final song first, it plays together for everyone with sound on, and guests can tell the reveal is close. Once the reveal has finished, a guest who taps the link later can still see the result and watch the reveal again.

Making it easy for grandparents

Grandparents are often the most excited guests, but installing a new app can be hard for them. PinkOrBlue has no app and no sign-up; it doesn't ask for an email or phone number. Tapping the link and typing a name is all it takes, plus the room password if the host set one. Sending the link a few days early and opening it together once is a good rehearsal: they see the countdown screen and can tap "Add to calendar" to download a calendar file and add it to their calendar. On reveal day, if someone is with them, have that person check that the sound is on and the phone isn't on silent.

Using it alongside a video call

PinkOrBlue doesn't have its own video calling, but it works well alongside a video call app. One way is to keep the call running on a laptop or tablet and open PinkOrBlue on a phone, so everyone watches the reveal and sees each other's reactions. Running both on one device makes the screen small, and the sounds can mix. If the music echoes on the call, one side can mute their microphone. After the reveal, you can use the share to your story card to send the result to loved ones who couldn't make the call. That way, even those who missed it live can still feel part of the day.

Frequently asked questions

How do guests in another country see the reveal time?

The reveal date and time are shown on each guest's screen in their own device's time. The countdown runs to the same moment for everyone, so guests in different time zones watch the reveal at the same time as you.

Do relatives overseas need to install anything?

No. A phone, tablet or computer with an internet connection and a browser is enough. Guests tap the link, type their name and join the room; if it has a password, they enter it first. There's no app to download and no sign-up.

Can grandparents who aren't tech-savvy join?

Yes. Joining happens in the browser: once the party room opens, guests type their name, and password-protected rooms ask for the password first. Opening the link together once ahead of time, and having someone nearby on reveal day, makes it even easier.

How do I use PinkOrBlue with a video call?

The easiest way is to run the video call on one device and PinkOrBlue on another. That way you watch the reveal and see each other's reactions at the same time. If the sounds mix, one side can mute their microphone.

What happens if my internet drops during the reveal?

Tap the link again once you're back online; on the same browser, your name and vote are kept. If the reveal is still running, you join it at the current point, not where you left off. If it has finished, you'll see the result and the guesses, and can tap "Watch the reveal again."
