How to host a virtual gender reveal party

Your loved ones don't need to be in the same room to share the big moment. Here's how to plan an online gender reveal, from the first invite to the final pop.

What is a virtual gender reveal?

At a virtual gender reveal, family and friends find out whether the baby is a boy or a girl together, but each from their own screen. There's no venue, decorations or food to plan; guests join from home, from work or from another city. You can also pair it with a small party at home and bring everyone who lives far away into the same moment. With PinkOrBlue you open a party room and share the link; guests vote Boy or Girl, and when you start the reveal, the result opens on everyone's screen together. It's free, with no app to download and no sign-up.

Planning: date, time and guest list

Your first decision is the date and time of the reveal. Pick a time when most of your guests are at home and relaxed; weekends and evenings usually work well. Ask your doctor when and how you can learn the result, and schedule the party after that. Then make your guest list: close family, friends, maybe a few coworkers. PinkOrBlue was built with family-and-friends gatherings of around 30 people in mind; that's not a cap on guests. When you set up the room, you choose the date and time of the reveal, and guests see the time left as a countdown.

Who keeps the secret?

The biggest question of the party is who knows the result. Some parents find out first and save the surprise for their guests. Others want to be surprised too, so they hand the result to someone they trust. In PinkOrBlue, the person who sets up the room enters the result secretly by default. If you'd rather not know, open "More settings" and pass the right to enter the result and start the reveal to someone who has joined the room. That person becomes the secret keeper, and you find out together with everyone else. The result stays sealed, never shows on guests' screens, and can be changed until the reveal.

How the reveal moment flows

Until 30 minutes before the reveal, guests who tap the link see a countdown screen. Then the party room opens: you can see who has voted, who hasn't yet, and who is on the page right now, by name. While they wait, guests can compete in a mini game called Balloon Pop. If the host taps "Start the final song," it plays together for everyone with sound on; the tip is to start the reveal 10 seconds before it ends. Once the reveal begins, everyone taps their screen to blow up a giant balloon together. When it pops, pink or blue appears on every open screen together, though connections can add a slight delay.

Tips for a great guest experience

Put the reveal date and time clearly in your invite message; on the countdown screen, guests can tap "Add to calendar" to download a calendar file and add it to their calendar, with a reminder 30 minutes before the reveal. Remind everyone to turn their sound on. Start the reveal when everyone has arrived; the screen of the person starting it shows how many people have voted and how many are on the page right now. Afterward, the results screen shows who called it, Team Pink and Team Blue, the guesses, plus messages and name ideas. You can share the result with a card, and anyone who missed it can watch the reveal again.

Frequently asked questions

Do guests need an app for a virtual gender reveal?

No. PinkOrBlue runs in the browser on a phone, tablet or computer. Guests tap the link and type their name; if the room has a password, they enter the one the host shared first. There's no sign-up, and no email address or phone number is asked for.

Can we host a gender reveal if we don't know the result either?

Yes. The person who sets up the room can pass the right to enter the result and start the reveal to someone trusted who has joined the room. That secret keeper enters the result and starts the reveal, and the parents find out with everyone else.

What if a guest isn't on the page during the reveal?

The reveal plays together for everyone who has the page open. Once the reveal has finished, a guest who taps the link later still sees the result, who called it and the guesses, and can tap "Watch the reveal again." If a reveal was cut short, the result may not appear right away.

How many people can join a virtual gender reveal?

PinkOrBlue was built with family-and-friends gatherings of around 30 people in mind. That number isn't a limit on guests, just the group size the design is aimed at. Guests can also send the link to anyone who was left out.

How long is the party data kept?

The room closes 7 days after the reveal date chosen at setup, and its names, votes, guesses and scores are deleted shortly after. Before the reveal starts, the host can delete the room and all its data from "More settings." There are no ads and no tracking cookies.
